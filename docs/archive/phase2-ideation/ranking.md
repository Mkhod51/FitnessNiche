## Notes where a score needs defending

- **SBL-2 Evid 5 / Seg 5:** the recomp/deficit physiology is [A/B] and the segment's WTP is *measured*, not guessed — it's the rare candidate strong on both.
- **SBL-6 Diff 5 but Risk 3:** the "designed to end" inversion is the only genuinely incumbent-proof moat in the set (a business can't copy a product that tells users to stop paying), but a cut-branded product sits closest to the ED line — the capped, bounded, performance-framed design is load-bearing mitigation, hence Risk 3 not 5.
- **SBL-5 Evid 2:** the reverse-arrow is the most *novel* idea (Diff 5) and the most under-evidenced (Evid 2). For this audience that combination is fatal — it asks the honesty-valuing niche to trust a [C] nudge. Ranked last deliberately; it's the boundary of what the niche will accept.
- **SBL-1 Feas 5 / Risk 5:** cheapest, safest build; capped at 4.22 because "honestly does less than RP" is a thin standalone value prop even if it's a superb *component*.
- **SBL-4, SBL-3, SBL-8 all score as components, not products** — they are the engine (SBL-4), the presentation (SBL-3), and the brand principle (SBL-8) of the same thing SBL-2/SBL-6 are.

## Structural read (outranks the arithmetic, as in Phase 1)

Five of the eight candidates converge: SBL-2's reconciliation engine needs SBL-4's honest e1RM, presents naturally as SBL-3's block report, is branded by SBL-8's evidence layer, and reaches its cleanest, most defensible form as SBL-6's bounded cut. The set is really **one product with a strategic fork**:

- **SBL-2** — the always-on reconciler. Higher LTV, broader use (cut *and* bulk), but competes more directly with a MacroFactor that could add training.
- **SBL-6** — the same engine as a bounded, incumbent-proof cut companion. Narrower, lower LTV, but a moat no incumbent will cross and a cleaner interview story ("I built the app to be finished").

The genuine decision is SBL-2 vs SBL-6. Resolved in `recommended.md`.
